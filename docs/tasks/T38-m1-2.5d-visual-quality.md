## Outcome and scope

Improve the current bright illustrated mystery game's perceived craft and animation quality while retaining the existing Phaser sprite-based 2.5D renderer. The sample should show clearer character silhouettes/poses and more tactile depth in the existing office art. Art remains locally reproducible through the existing SVG generators; the editable SVG pipeline is a better fit than adding raster tooling for this pass.

Keep the T37 story, React DOM learning/UI content, API and case content, world coordinates, collision geometry, scanner timing, scoring, input and checkpoint rules unchanged. No new dependencies, paid assets, 3D renderer, deployment or external participants. AI roleplay is simulated feedback only.

## Design decisions

- Preserve approved M0 art direction: warm, hand-finished painted 2.5D, bright mystery palette, teal shadows, expressive character acting and restrained clue lighting.
- Improve the reproducible vector pipeline directly: stronger lighting/material gradients and edge/shadow treatment for current room/props; more individually readable clothing, hair and facial expressions; clearer anticipation/action/recovery in existing animation states.
- Keep DOM text primary and readable. Respect reduced motion, asset fallback, depth sorting, canvas focus, and all stable 80×120 frame foot anchors unless a tested art-only size adjustment is justified.
- Do not copy the generic UI/UX Pro Max landing-page or parallax search recommendations; they did not fit an in-canvas detective game. No generated raster files are planned.

## Five AI learner-role script (after art pass)

Run five independent agent turns against the same final build and same short first-play scenario; do not show agents one another's notes or tell them the intended answer. Each receives one role: (1) A2 job seeker who leans on Vietnamese only when stuck; (2) A2 office newcomer who wants short instructions; (3) B1 office worker who notices version language; (4) casual mystery player unfamiliar with action games; (5) casual browser-game player who is skeptical of educational overlays. Ask each to narrate likely first actions, paraphrase the clue/theory, describe what the visual acting/action communicated, say where they hesitate, and choose whether they would continue with a concrete reason. Collect strengths, confusion points and one fix in the same template. Do not ask for or infer real comprehension, enjoyment, retention, or willingness to pay from roleplay. Report convergence and disagreement as hypotheses; never mix these with human scorecard evidence.
