## Method

Five independent subagent turns roleplayed: (1) an A2 job seeker, (2) an A2 office newcomer who wants short directions, (3) a B1 office worker who watches version wording, (4) a casual mystery player unfamiliar with action games, and (5) a casual browser-game player skeptical of educational overlays. Each used the same first-play sequence: E01 → E02 → scanner dodge → E03 → Nora E06. They were instructed not to run or change the game, inspect answer keys, or share findings. They reviewed public-facing case/spec/UI/art material and the same headed captures. Static screenshots support visual observations only; they cannot show animation timing or interaction discovery in motion.

The final normal-room captures are preserved in [`T38-before.png`](assets/T38-before.png) and [`T38-after.png`](assets/T38-after.png). A temporary missing-art fallback capture from the headed run is not used as evidence of normal art.

## Synthesis

### Where the roles converged

- Most would follow the Vietnamese mission, highlighted EMAIL marker, and `E` prompt first. The bright floor split, props, clue labels and coral interactable rings make the room legible.
- The English evidence can create a version-mix-up question. The agents generally wanted to reach Nora to learn whether the change was a mistake; they did not treat the account/time clue alone as motive.
- Optional Vietnamese supports an English-first read. A2 roles asked for shorter Vietnamese task prompts near dense evidence; the casual player worried repeated translation decisions could interrupt play.
- The scanner appears to add a useful change of pace, but a static image cannot establish whether its sweep and dodge timing are clear to a first-time player. Roleplay recommendations asked for a short, explicit `Space` instruction and a legible safe window.
- The visual pass reads as a coherent, brighter illustrated office with layered floor and prop shading. NPC color/clothing help distinguish characters, but small gameplay sprites still have simple faces; body acting and expression need a live-motion check.

### Useful differences

- The A2 newcomer is most concerned about long English paragraphs and suggested concise, contextual Vietnamese objectives while keeping original English visible.
- The B1 office worker wants a revisitable, fact-only version timeline so that “approved report” and “client file” do not read as contradictory evidence.
- The casual mystery player prioritizes a clear scanner instruction and a character-led reveal.
- The casual browser-game player sees a risk that the optional working-theory note feels like homework; it should remain clearly skippable and must not interrupt access to E03 or Nora.
- The A2 job seeker expects to use translation only when stuck and wants the danger state to remain readable without sound.

## Limits

These agents are text models roleplaying plausible perspectives. They did not measure comprehension, accessibility, enjoyment, retention, willingness to pay, or actual completion. Their theory paraphrases are not learner scores. Do not use the five reports to claim market demand or to skip the owner's planned 5–8 real learner sessions.

## Product implication

Recommend M2 as a bounded player-validation milestone: first clear the target-device and consent/retention gates, then run the owner-led learner sessions using the existing scorecard and agree pass criteria in advance. Observe scanner discovery/retry, translation use, clue interpretation, whether the note feels optional, and who wants to continue. Use those observations and an art/audio cost estimate to choose a second-case scope. Do not start paid case production or checkout from these AI simulations alone.
